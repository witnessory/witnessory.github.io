Post-quantum key exchange is at 59%. Post-quantum signatures are at 0%.

The internet has moved half of its encryption to post-quantum key exchange, mostly through browser and server updates nobody had to think about. It has not moved its signatures at all: not on certificates, DNSSEC, mail or routing. This is what Witnessory measured this month, and why the second number is the one to watch.

Why the split matters

The two halves defend against different attacks, on different clocks.

Key exchange defends against record now, decrypt later: someone stores today's encrypted traffic and opens it once a large enough quantum computer exists. The fix only needs the two ends of a connection to agree, so it ships as a software update. Browsers, large CDNs and OpenSSH did exactly that, and the web reached 59% without most site operators doing anything.

Signatures defend against impersonation: a forged certificate, a forged DNS answer, a forged route, a forged update. Recorded traffic is of no use for that, so the danger starts only when the machine exists. But the fix is far slower. A post-quantum certificate is useless until browsers trust a post-quantum root; a post-quantum DNSSEC zone needs an algorithm number that does not exist yet; DKIM has no post-quantum key type registered. Each needs a standard, then the ecosystem that relies on it, then every operator. And it has to be finished before the machine exists, because a signature that can be forged on that day protects nothing.

So the 0% is not neglect: in most of these systems there is not yet a standard to deploy. It is the measure of how much is still to come, and the reason to watch it from the first non-zero reading.

What else turned up

  • Mail signing is behind before quantum even arrives. In one probe's reading of 29 October, of the 771 DKIM keys found for the top 1,000 sites, 395 are RSA-1024 and 358 RSA-2048. Eleven are RSA-768 and one is RSA-512, sizes that classical computers have already factored. (Selectors cannot be listed; the probe asks for 50 common ones.)
  • SSH moved early, on the pre-standard algorithm. 51.3% of SSH servers offer a post-quantum hybrid (50.2% to 52.3% across four networks), but only 16.2% offer ML-KEM, the standardized one. Most offer sntrup761, which OpenSSH shipped first. The population is small: 305 to 348 SSH servers among the top 10,000 domains, depending on the network.
  • Mail transport is close behind the web. 97.5% of the top 1,000 sites' primary mail servers offer STARTTLS, and 45.1% of those negotiate post-quantum key exchange (42.2% and 47.9% on the two networks where port 25 was reachable).
  • Encrypted Client Hello is not post-quantum. 5.5% of the top sites checked publish an ECH key, and none of those keys is post-quantum, so the hidden site name can still be recovered from a recording later.
  • One software update channel already signs with ML-DSA. Of 16 channels watched (Linux distributions, package registries, a firmware service), one signs with ML-DSA-87 alongside RSA. Thirteen sign with RSA-4096.
  • The trust machinery held. Every transparency log checked (certificates, software signing, the Go checksum database) proved its history consistent across 468 heads, with no split views between probes; no TLS interception was seen at any probe.

How this was measured

  • Four probes on five networks, residential and data-centre. Each probe signs every reading twice (Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65) and chains it to the one before; the chain is anchored in Bitcoin with OpenTimestamps, so no reading can be changed or back-dated without it showing.
  • Every figure here is read from one view, a deterministic computation over the readings, named by its hash: c15ae91fb82de16ad92971c7de2552291c2eed10079ac9d1a4ce657b6ff436e0, as of 2026-10-30 07:01 UTC. The view is published; the readings and the aggregator are to be published, and until then the hash is the record.
  • Rates are of what answered. Sites that time out or refuse are counted apart, not as failures. The web figure is over the sites that answered: about 8,200 of the Tranco top 10,000 on a full run.

Preliminary: the probes are younger than the 30 days after which they carry full weight. Five networks is a thin basis, and the ranges will move as probes on more networks join.
